# Gap re-verification — 2026-08-16

The project brief's first instruction was to re-verify the gap before writing code, because the
underlying research was dated 2026-08-14 and its own core lesson is that packaging gaps close
in weeks. This is that check, recorded so it can be disputed.

**Verdict: the gap is open. Proceed — but the framing has to change.**

## What was searched

Live web search, 2026-08-16: cross-engine OCR hallucination diff tooling; GitHub for
verification tools comparing Tesseract against VLM output; Marker/MinerU/olmOCR QA and CI
tooling; PyPI for OCR consensus/verification packages; Show HN and Product Hunt for launched
tools; the `awesome-ocr-2026` list's tooling sections.

## Nothing ships this

No tool found takes `PDF + a VLM engine's output` and returns per-page, image-backed
divergences. `awesome-ocr-2026` has no verification/QA tooling section at all — it covers model
selection, leaderboards and deployment. The closest listed resources measure OCR accuracy
against ground truth, which is a different problem: benchmarking tells you how an engine scores
on someone else's corpus, not whether *your* page 47 was fabricated.

## Three adjacents, none of them the same tool

1. **`consensus-entropy`** — [repo](https://github.com/Aslan-yulong/consensus-entropy),
   [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11101) (CVPR 2026). The nearest thing to a competitor and
   the most important find. Same core thesis: correct predictions converge, errors diverge.
   But it ships a **scoring function over candidate strings** — no bounding boxes, no crops, no
   report, no CLI, no PDF handling; 10 stars, MIT. It also needs *multiple VLM runs* to
   generate its candidates (GPU, cost per page) where this tool needs one cheap deterministic
   CPU witness. Its README concedes the key limitation: agreement is not proof of correctness.

2. **olmOCR-Bench** — penalizes hallucinated and repeated n-grams under "long text robustness".
   A benchmark against ground truth, not per-document verification of a live run.

3. **Engine-internal mitigations** — Marker fixes table cells that cut text lines to reduce
   hallucination; `local-llm-pdf-ocr` has a dense-page mode that avoids the loops full-page OCR
   exhibits. These are engines defending themselves, not independent verification.

## What changed since 2026-08-14 — and it matters for the pitch

The *concept* has spread into practitioner blog posts in the months since. "Run critical fields
through two models and flag disagreements" and "cross-check financial figures with a traditional
OCR pass" now appear as standard advice in 2026 OCR guides. With `consensus-entropy` accepted at
CVPR 2026, the idea also has an academic blessing and a citable name.

The consequence: **the pitch cannot be "nobody thought of this."** It has to be "everybody
recommends this and nobody packaged it." That is a weaker novelty claim but a stronger demand
signal — the advice existing without a tool implementing it is precisely the wedge. It also
means the window is narrower than the original research assumed, and that a follow-up tool
could plausibly come out of the consensus-entropy group.

## One design decision this changed

The multi-VLM consensus framing is a genuine technical rival, and on *degraded* pages it is
arguably stronger: Tesseract also fails badly there, so a witness disagreement is ambiguous
between "the AI fabricated" and "the witness failed."

The asymmetry that keeps the witness design sound is about *how* each engine fails, not how
often: Tesseract fails **visibly** (garbage, or nothing), VLMs fail **invisibly** (fluent
prose). So the design leans hardest on the case where that asymmetry is total — blank or
near-blank pages, where the witness cannot be wrong about the absence of ink — and hedges
everywhere the witness itself is shaky, rather than pretending to be an oracle. Hence
`witness_quality: low` on the page, severity damping on its findings, and a crop attached to
every claim.

*Addendum, same day, after the adversarial study and red-team round:* the hedging principle
survived contact with a real engine but its first implementation did not. The witness's
visible failures turn out to have a measurable signature — short shredded tokens — and that
signature, not disagreement ratios alone, is now what licenses a hedge; symmetric
disagreement without shreds is treated as the rewrite it resembles. And hedges are budgeted
in CI, because "cannot verify" must never become a free pass. Details in study/README.md.
